# Al-Fatihah: A Case Study

A series of close-reading case studies on the Quran, working through the text Ayah by Ayah. Each study draws on classical Arabic lexicology, rhetorical analysis, classical Tafsir, and where relevant, philosophy, psychology, and intellectual history. The approach is not comparative religion. It is sustained, careful reading — the kind that takes the text seriously on its own terms before looking anywhere else.

## How to read

Start with the brief overview. It tells you what the study covers and what to expect. The full case study, available on demand, is the main work — it goes through the Surah in sequence, Ayah by Ayah, covering:

- The name, classification, and central theme of the Surah
- Lexical insights — root analysis and word-level precision
- Linguistic and rhetorical beauty — grammar, syntax, and rhetorical features
- Quranic resonance — how the Ayah connects to other places in the Quran
- Philosophical horizons — where relevant thinkers illuminate the territory
- Psychological reflections — what empirical research has found in the same space
- Historical and social contexts where they add depth

## A note on the approach

These studies do not assume the reader is a scholar. I do assumed the reader is someone who is deeply willing to read carefully. The Quran is treated as a text that rewards attention — linguistically, rhetorically, and intellectually — and the study try to demonstrate that rather than assert it.

Arabic terms are retained and explained rather than replaced with English equivalents, because the English equivalents are almost always inadequate. "Lord" does not mean Rabb. "Praise" does not mean Hamd. The lexical work is part of the study.
