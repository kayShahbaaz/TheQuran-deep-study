## What this repo contains

This repository holds the public-facing materials for an original research study on the intersections between Quranic Ayaat and the empirical findings of cyberpsychology. The study is not a spiritual commentary. It is an academic cross-mapping: specific Ayaat, specific constructs, specific researchers, specific papers, specific data.

## The study in one paragraph

Twenty Ayaat from the Quran map with precision onto the core constructs of cyberpsychology: online disinhibition, misinformation propagation, confirmation bias, social comparison, behavioral addiction, cognitive offloading, self-regulation failure, digital identity plasticity, hate propagation, and the epistemology of rumor. Each connection is grounded in named researchers, peer-reviewed papers, stated methodologies, and documented findings — not metaphor or analogy, not surface-level quotation. The Quran did not predict the internet. It described the human being. Cyberpsychology is now documenting what that description predicts.

## Structure of the research

The study runs in two parts.

**Part I** covers fourteen Ayaat, each mapped to a specific cyberpsychology construct with full methodology and findings: online disinhibition (49:12), epistemic accountability (17:36), confirmation bias (2:170), social comparison (102:1–8), behavioral addiction (96:6–7), cognitive offloading (3:190–191), self-regulation (59:18), digital identity (91:7–10), diseased heart (2:10), rumor epistemology (49:6), cyberbullying (49:11), observational learning (18:28), connected loneliness (30:21), and temporal loss (103:1–3).

**Part II** goes deeper: the three-stage Quranic model of the self (nafs al-ammara, nafs al-lawwama, nafs al-mutma'inna) mapped against Kahneman's dual-process theory and self-determination research; the Festinger-Takathur Loop as a unified mechanism; what cyberpsychology cannot provide and where the Quranic framework fills the gap; and five directions for future empirical research.
