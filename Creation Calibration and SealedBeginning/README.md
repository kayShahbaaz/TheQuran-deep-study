# Creation, Calibration, and the Sealed Beginning

### A Qurʾānic Study of Thirteen Āyāt on the Origin, Structure, and Constitution of the Universe

*LearnQurʾān: Beneath the Āyah series*

---

## What this repository is

This repository is the public-facing record of a long-form study that moves through thirteen Āyāt across nine Sūrahs of the Qurʾān, tracing the Arabic words for creation, calibration, the sealed beginning of the universe, the expansion of the sky, the origin of all life in water, and the divine act that brought everything into existence from nothing.

The study sits at the intersection of classical Arabic lexicology, Qurʾānic exegesis, and the history of modern cosmology. It draws on scholars including Al-Rāghib Al-Iṣfahānī رحمه الله, Ibn Manẓūr, Ibn ʿĀshūr رحمه الله, Ibn Al-Qayyim رحمه الله, and Al-Ṭabarī رحمه الله, and on the published work of physicists including Martin Rees, Steven Weinberg, Stephen Hawking, Roger Penrose, and Alexander Vilenkin.

This is not a "Qurʾān predicted science" piece. That framing does a disservice to both. The study is a careful, word-by-word reading of what the Qurʾān says, alongside an equally careful reading of what the universe, studied as rigorously as human beings have ever studied anything, reveals. Neither is forced onto the other. Both are allowed to speak.

Sometimes two people standing in completely different places are looking at the same thing.

## A note on the approach

Every Arabic root analysis in this study is grounded in classical sources, primarily Al-Rāghib Al-Iṣfahānī's رحمه الله *Mufradāt Alfāẓ Al-Qurʾān* and Ibn Manẓūr's *Lisān Al-ʿArab*, rather than in modern translated meanings alone. The gap between the two is often considerable. What the Qurʾān says with a word is frequently more precise than any English rendering of that word carries across.

Every scientific claim is sourced to the original paper or publication. Where a physicist is cited, the full reference is given. Nothing is paraphrased beyond what the source says.

The study is written to be read by someone with no prior background in Arabic or in physics. Technical terms are explained when they first appear. Arabic is transliterated throughout.
