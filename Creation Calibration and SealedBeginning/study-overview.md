# Creation, Calibration, and the Sealed Beginning

### A Qurʾānic Study of Thirteen Āyāt on the Origin, Structure, and Constitution of the Universe

---

There is a question that has a way of arriving uninvited.

Not in lectures or labs or in the middle of conversations but in the spaces between things. The moment after the light goes off and the room goes completely dark. A night when sleep will not come and the ceiling above you is just there, existing for no reason you can name.

The mind slides past all the familiar surfaces. Past the names of things, their functions, their explanations. And it lands somewhere that has no floor.

*Why is there anything?*

Before the first question of science or philosophy or theology was ever asked: why is there something rather than nothing? What is existence resting on?

Every physicist who has spent serious time at the frontier of cosmology knows this feeling, not as a moment of weakness, but as the inevitable destination of following the evidence all the way to where it runs out. This study begins there.

---

## Scope

The study examines five questions across thirteen Āyāt:

**1. The meaning of creation**

The Arabic root خ-ل-ق, the root of *khalaqa*, the word the Qurʾān uses for the creation of the heavens and the earth, carries within it the sense of precise measurement and calibrated proportion, not simply making. The Qurʾān returns to this across Sūrah Al-Qamar (54:49), Sūrah Al-Furqān (25:2), and Sūrah Al-Aʿlā (87:2-3). What that precision means sits alongside Martin Rees's documentation of the six fundamental constants of nature and Steven Weinberg's identification of the cosmological constant problem as the most difficult problem in theoretical physics.

**2. The prior state of the heavens and the earth**

Sūrah Al-Anbiyāʾ (21:30) describes the heavens and the earth as once having been *ratqan*, a word that in classical Arabic means sealed so thoroughly that no gap, seam, or differentiation of any kind existed. The study reads this word against the work of Georges Lemaître, Edwin Hubble, and Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson, whose discovery of the cosmic microwave background radiation gave the beginning of the universe its thermal echo.

**3. The ongoing expansion**

Sūrah Al-Dhāriyāt (51:47) uses the present tense: *We are its expanders*. The tense is not incidental. The study reads this against Hubble's 1929 measurements and what they imply about a universe still moving outward in every direction as these words are read.

**4. What living things are made of**

Sūrah Al-Anbiyāʾ (21:30), Sūrah Al-Nūr (24:45), and Sūrah Al-Furqān (25:54) state across three separate revelations that every living thing, and specifically the human being, was made *from* water. The preposition matters: not sustained by it, but made from it. The study reads this against the biochemistry of the cell, the role of water in protein folding, membrane function, DNA replication, and the question scientists ask first when searching for life elsewhere in the universe.

**5. The nature of the creative act**

Sūrah Al-Baqarah (2:117) names Allah as *Badīʿ* of the heavens and the earth, a word whose root *badaʿa* *ibdaʿa* means to bring something into existence with no prior example, no material, no template. In other words, to bring something into existence from nothing. The study reads this name against the published work of Stephen Hawking and James Hartle on the no-boundary proposal, Roger Penrose on the initial conditions of the universe, and Alexander Vilenkin on creation from nothing, paying particular attention to what each of these physicists, working at the limit of what physics can say, found themselves unable to answer.

## Methodology

Every Arabic root analysis is grounded in classical lexicological sources:

- Al-Rāghib Al-Iṣfahānī رحمه الله, *Mufradāt Alfāẓ Al-Qurʾān*
- Ibn Manẓūr, *Lisān Al-ʿArab*
- Al-Ṭabarī رحمه الله, *Jāmiʿ Al-Bayān*
- Ibn ʿĀshūr رحمه الله, *Al-Taḥrīr wa Al-Tanwīr*
- Ibn Al-Qayyim رحمه الله, *Madārij Al-Sālikīn*
- Sayyid Quṭb رحمه الله, *Fī Ẓilāl Al-Qurʾān*

Every scientific claim is sourced to the original paper or publication. Where a physicist is cited, the citation is given in full.

The study is written to be accessible to someone with no prior background in Arabic or in physics. Technical terms are explained on first appearance. Arabic transliteration follows a consistent scholarly system throughout.

---

## What the study is not

It does not argue that the Qurʾān predicted modern science. That framing collapses distinctions that matter: between description and prediction, between precision and coincidence, between what a word carries and what a translation of it carries.

What the study does is simpler and, in the end, more unsettling. It reads carefully in two directions at once. It asks what the Qurʾān says when read in the language it was revealed in, with the precision the classical scholars brought to it. Then it asks what the universe says when examined with the precision the best physicists have brought to it. Both answers are allowed to sit in the same room.

Sometimes two people standing in completely different places are looking at the same thing.
