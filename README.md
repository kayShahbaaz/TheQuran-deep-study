# TheQurʾān: Through Its Language

## LearnQurʾān: Beneath the Āyah

> Linguistics · Philosophy · Science 

---


This repository is home of **Beneath the Āyah**, a growing series of Qurʾānic studies by **kayShahbaaz**.

The project began with a simple conviction: the Qurʾān deserves to be read slowly. Not only for its broad themes or familiar lessons, but for the exact word chosen, the grammatical movement of a sentence, the order in which an idea appears, and the questions that open when the text is allowed to remain precise.

Each study takes a passage seriously on its own terms. It begins with the Arabic, listens to the classical scholars, and then follows the passage into the wider questions it raises. Depending on the subject, that wider field may include physics, cosmology, psychology, cyberpsychology, philosophy, history, or the ordinary structure of human life.

The purpose is not to make the Qurʾān conform to a modern theory. It is not to collect impressive similarities between revelation and contemporary research. It is a more careful exercise than that: to read both the Qurʾān and the world honestly, and to notice what becomes visible when neither one is reduced to a slogan.

Sometimes two people standing in completely different places are looking at the same thing.

## The studies

### [Al-Fātiḥah: A Case Study](Al-Fatihah%20-%20CaseStudy/)

Al-Fātiḥah is read Āyah by Āyah, beginning with Taʿawwudh and Basmalah and ending with a note on Āmīn. The study examines the Surah's roots, grammar, rhetorical structure, Qurʾānic resonances, and spiritual movement.

It also follows four larger questions: the linguistic pivot at the centre of the Surah, why Al-Fātiḥah is Umm al-Kitāb, how the early Sufi masters of Ahl al-Sunnah read it inwardly, and how the opening of Al-Baqarah answers the supplication made in Al-Fātiḥah.

This is a study of a text recited every day, approached with the patience it has always deserved.

### [Al-ʿAṣr: Time, Entropy, and the Architecture of Exception](Al-Asr%20-%20Time%20Entropy%20and%20the%20Architecture%20of%20Exception/)

Sūrah Al-ʿAṣr is only three Āyāt, yet Imām Al-Shāfiʿī رحمه الله described it as sufficient for human guidance. This study takes that claim seriously.

It begins with the oath by *al-ʿaṣr*, follows the meaning of *khusr* through classical Arabic lexicography, and places that diagnosis beside the history and structure of entropy. The comparison is deliberately limited: *khusr* does not mean entropy, and the Qurʾān is not being presented as a physics textbook. The study asks instead what it means for both descriptions to identify an irreversible loss of foundational capacity.

The final Āyah is then read as an architecture of exception: *īmān*, *ʿamal ṣāliḥ*, and the reciprocal holding expressed by *tawāṣaw*.

### [Al-Kahf: The Cure for Materialism](Al-Kahf%20-%20The%20Cure%20for%20Materialism/)

Sūrah Al-Kahf is prescribed for every Friday. This study asks why a Surah would be returned to weekly, across an entire lifetime.

Its answer is that Al-Kahf is not merely warning against greed. It is treating a perceptual error: the narrowing of reality to what can be seen, possessed, measured, and accumulated. The People of the Cave, the Two Garden Owners, Mūsā and Al-Khiḍr عليهما السلام, and Dhū Al-Qarnayn each expose a different form of that error.

The study reads the Surah through the classical tradition, with particular attention to the idea of *zīnah*, and alongside research into wealth, insulation from consequence, and the limits of human perception.

### [Creation, Calibration, and the Sealed Beginning](Creation%20Calibration%20and%20SealedBeginning/)

This study follows thirteen Āyāt across the Qurʾān and asks five connected questions: what creation means, what the heavens and earth were before differentiation, whether the universe is still expanding, what living things are made from, and what it means for Allah to bring something into existence without prior example.

The Arabic roots are read through classical lexicography and Tafsīr. The cosmological questions are set beside the published work of Lemaître, Hubble, Penzias and Wilson, Hawking, Penrose, Vilenkin, and others. The study does not argue that the Qurʾān predicted modern science. It keeps description, prediction, and interpretation distinct, because those distinctions matter.

### [Qurʾānic Āyāt and Cyberpsychology](Quranic%20Ayaat%20and%20Cyberpsychology/)

This is an academic cross-mapping of twenty Āyāt to specific constructs in cyberpsychology and related fields. The study considers online disinhibition, misinformation, confirmation bias, social comparison, behavioural addiction, cognitive offloading, self-regulation, digital identity, rumor, cyberbullying, connected loneliness, and temporal loss.

The aim is precision: a specific Āyah, a specific psychological construct, a named researcher, a specific paper, and a clear account of what the data showed. The Qurʾān did not predict the internet. It described the human being, and cyberpsychology is now documenting many of the mechanisms that description makes visible.

The second part develops a Qurʾānic model of the self through *nafs al-ammārah*, *nafs al-lawwāmah*, and *nafs al-muṭmaʾinnah*, and considers what a teleological framework can contribute where empirical psychology can describe behaviour but cannot determine what the human being is for.

## How the studies are written

### Begin with the word

Arabic terms are retained because translation is often not an equivalent exchange. A word such as *rabb*, *ḥamd*, *zīnah*, or *khusr* carries a field of meaning that a single English word cannot hold by itself. Roots, forms, syntax, and rhetorical choices are therefore treated as part of the argument, not as decoration around it.

### Listen to the classical tradition

The studies draw on the major works of Arabic lexicography, Tafsīr, theology, and spiritual scholarship. Among the recurring sources are Al-Rāghib Al-Iṣfahānī's *Mufradāt Alfāẓ Al-Qurʾān*, Ibn Manẓūr's *Lisān Al-ʿArab*, Al-Ṭabarī, Al-Zamakhsharī, Al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Al-Qayyim, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Sayyid Quṭb رحمه الله. The sources are not used to decorate a conclusion already reached; they are part of the process by which the conclusion is tested.

### Engage modern knowledge honestly

Where a study enters physics, cosmology, neuroscience, psychology, or cyberpsychology, it follows the relevant research and names the limits of the comparison. Scientific claims should be traceable to their publications. Qurʾānic claims should remain grounded in the language and scholarly tradition of the text.

### Keep the reader in view

No prior knowledge of Arabic, physics, or psychology is assumed. Technical terms are explained when they first appear. The studies are written for readers who are willing to slow down, not for readers who need to accept a conclusion before seeing how it was reached.

## What this repository contains

Each folder contains two public documents:

- `README.md` introduces the study and its place in the wider series.
- `study-overview.md` gives a fuller account of the study's question, scope, sources, and central argument.

The complete studies are available as formatted Word and PDF documents on request. They are not uploaded here because the full work is intended to remain in context rather than be scraped, reposted, or separated from its references and methodology.

The public written material in this repository is shared for personal reading, study, and educational use. Brief quotation with clear attribution is welcome.

Please do not reproduce or redistribute the complete studies, or use them commercially, without permission. The work is offered freely, but free access does not remove the responsibility to preserve authorship, context, and attribution.

## The larger project

This series is intended to grow into a study of all 114 Sūrahs of the Qurʾān, in Qurʾānic order, from Al-Fātiḥah to Al-Nās. The chapters will continue to move between the precision of the Arabic, the depth of the classical tradition, and the questions raised by the world in which we live.

The work is being built one passage at a time.
